## Overview

A two-layer fluid decomposes uniquely into a **barotropic mode** (the depth
mean, obeying exactly Ch. 7/18's ordinary barotropic vorticity equation)
and a **baroclinic mode** (the inter-layer difference, obeying exactly
Ch. 8's single-layer QG equation, and carrying all the available potential
energy). This chapter derives that exact decoupling, then breaks it on
purpose: seeding a disturbance purely in the baroclinic mode and watching
nonlinear self-advection leak a barotropic component into existence where
none was placed.

## The model

Notebook: `notebooks/ch10_two-layer-qg.py` → exported to
`/nb/ch10_two-layer-qg/`. Governing equations use the symbols in
[notation]({{< relref "notation.md" >}}).

$$q_{bt}=\frac{q_1+q_2}{2}=\nabla^2\psi_{bt},\qquad
  q_{bc}=\frac{q_1-q_2}{2}=\nabla^2\psi_{bc}-2F\psi_{bc}.$$

`gfdlib.baroclinic.to_modes`/`from_modes` convert between layer space and
mode space; `pv_2layer` is the forward PV operator (exact inverse of
`invert_2layer`); the time evolution reuses `rhs_2layer` from Ch. 16
unchanged, with zero mean shear — an unforced initial-value problem, not
an instability calculation.

## Both fluids

- **Ocean:** newly-formed mesoscale eddies are overwhelmingly baroclinic at
  birth (generated by Ch. 16's instability, which draws only on APE); the
  slow barotropization this notebook's leak illustrates in miniature is a
  leading theory for how eddy energy ultimately escapes into the
  basin-scale, nearly-barotropic circulation.
- **Atmosphere:** the storm track's baroclinic eddies feed the
  barotropic-dominated jet stream through essentially this same nonlinear
  route — Ch. 18's inverse cascade needs a source, and baroclinic
  instability plus this mode-coupling leak is where its energy actually
  comes from.

## Exercises

1. *(analytic)* Starting from $q_1=\nabla^2\psi_1+F(\psi_2-\psi_1)$,
   $q_2=\nabla^2\psi_2+F(\psi_1-\psi_2)$, substitute
   $\psi_1=\psi_{bt}+\psi_{bc}$, $\psi_2=\psi_{bt}-\psi_{bc}$ and confirm by
   hand that the $F$ terms cancel in $q_1+q_2$ and double in $q_1-q_2$,
   reproducing the boxed result above.
2. *(computational)* Modify `ch10_two-layer-qg.py` to seed the initial
   condition purely in the **barotropic** mode instead ($q_{bc}(t{=}0)=0$).
   Does a baroclinic component leak into existence the same way, or does a
   purely barotropic vortex stay purely barotropic? (Hint: think about
   whether a purely barotropic flow's Jacobian can ever produce a nonzero
   $q_{bc}$ tendency, given $q_{bc}$'s own equation has no barotropic-mode
   source term.)
3. *(exploratory)* Track the ratio (barotropic KE)/(baroclinic KE + APE) at
   the final time as you vary $\beta$. Does stronger $\beta$ enhance or
   suppress the leak — and can you connect your answer to how $\beta$
   changes the baroclinic mode's own Rossby-wave dispersion (Ch. 8)?
